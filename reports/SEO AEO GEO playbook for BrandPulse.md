# Retrieval wins, hype loses: BrandPulse's playbook

AI visibility in 2026 comes down to two things. The first is retrieval: being findable in the search index each engine queries (Google for AI Overviews, AI Mode and Gemini; Bing plus OpenAI's own crawl for ChatGPT and Copilot), for the sub-queries the engine writes for itself. The second is reputation: being named often enough on trusted third-party pages that the engine sees a brand in the sources it reads and in its training memory. The strongest evidence points the same way across the board. A NeurIPS 2025 benchmark found that a page's retrieval rank outweighs content rewriting tricks. Off-site brand mentions correlate with AI Overview visibility three times as strongly as backlinks do. A controlled Ahrefs test found schema did not move AI citations. Google says llms.txt is unused. Measurement is the second half of the story. AI answers are so unstable that the same brand list repeats less than 1% of the time, so the only defensible metric is the share of answers that mention a brand, across a fixed question set, over repeated runs, with a stated margin of error. For BrandPulse this cuts both ways. The free audit's core metric (presence rate against named competitors) is the right one. But today's scorecard gives roughly 12 of 100 points to items that are hype or weakly evidenced (llms.txt, FAQ and general schema, linked social profiles). It runs each question once, and for local businesses it uses a prompt template written for manufacturers and exporters. Those are exactly the weaknesses the KP Webbate review pointed to. The fix is to rebuild the audit as a transparent measurement product: tailored and justified questions, 3–5 runs per question, Wilson ranges, a frozen question set with citation diffs, and a separate tier for verified technical findings. The service should then sell shipped work (listings, reviews, third-party placements, answer pages, re-measurement) with no promise of AI placements, which is both the credible agency norm and what Indian consumer-protection law requires.

## Google still ranks with links, clicks and quality, but AI Overviews now cite beyond the top ten

Google's own model is unchanged in outline: crawl, index, serve. Ranking comes from a set of named systems. The December 2025 ranking-systems guide lists them: link analysis and PageRank, passage ranking, neural matching, reliable information, original content, reviews, freshness, deduplication, site diversity and SpamBrain. Helpful Content, Panda and Penguin now live inside "core" rather than as standalone systems ([Google ranking systems guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide)). Google publishes no weights, but sworn testimony fills in an important gap. In the DOJ antitrust trial, Google VP Pandu Nayak testified that **click data has fed ranking since about 2005 through NavBoost**, using roughly 13 months of user-interaction data segmented by location and device. Trial exhibits describe "ABC" topicality signals (anchors, body, clicks) ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-abc-ranking-signals-455360); [Wikipedia on the 2024 leak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Google_Search_documentation_leak)). The 2024 Content Warehouse leak added attribute names such as `siteAuthority`, `siteFocusScore` and `siteRadius`, but no weights. Read them as hints that site-level authority and topical focus exist, not as a recipe. The practical reading for a small business is that searcher satisfaction, brand recognition that earns clicks, and a tightly focused site all compound. On-page tweaks alone do not.

E-E-A-T is a framework for human raters, not a ranking switch. Rater scores train and evaluate systems but do not change any individual site's position ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-quality-raters-content-ai-generated-454161)). The January 2025 rater-guideline update told raters to give **"Lowest" to main content mostly generated with little effort or originality**. Google's spam policies (last updated 28 August 2026) define scaled content abuse by intent, "many pages generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings", whatever the method. They also now name **"attempting to manipulate generative AI responses in Google Search"** as spam ([Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)). Core updates landed in March 2026 (27 March–8 April) and May 2026 (21 May–2 June) with no stated goals ([SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-begins-rolling-out-may-2026-core-update/575589/)). A June 2026 spam update targeted scaled content and site reputation abuse ([Priority Pixels](https://prioritypixels.co.uk/insights/googles-june-2026-spam-update-is-rolling-out/)). City-swap pages and programmatic AI blogs are now the riskiest tactics a client can buy.

For local businesses Google names only three factors: **relevance, distance and prominence**. Prominence explicitly includes review count and rating, and Google states "there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking" ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)). The technical baseline is stable and confirmed. Mobile-first indexing has been complete since July 2024. Googlebot renders JavaScript. Core Web Vitals are a light tiebreaker. FAQ rich results were restricted in 2023 to authoritative government and health sites ([Google, Aug 2023](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes)), and seven more rich-result types were retired in June 2025 ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/06/simplifying-search-results)). For most SMBs, FAQ schema therefore earns no visible SERP feature.

### AI Overviews and AI Mode retrieve by fan-out, so head-term rank is no longer enough

Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode "may use a 'query fan-out' technique — issuing multiple related searches across subtopics and data sources". A page needs only to be indexed and snippet-eligible. Google states plainly that there are "**no additional requirements… nor other special optimizations necessary**", "no special schema.org structured data", and no need for "AI text files" ([Google: AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). The data shows what fan-out does to citations. In July 2025, Ahrefs found **76% of AI Overview citations ranked in the organic top 10** for the query ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/search-rankings-ai-citations)). By March 2026, after AI Overviews moved to Gemini 3, its larger sample found **only 38%** did, with YouTube the single most-cited domain at 5.6% ([SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-ai-overview-citations-from-top-ranking-pages-drop-sharply/568637/)). Ahrefs cautions that parsing changes make the two datasets not directly comparable. Moz found 88% of AI Mode citations were absent from that query's organic results. Ranking first for "best CA firm in Pune" is now neither necessary nor sufficient. Ranking for the many narrower sub-questions, and being on the third-party pages that do rank for them, is what gets a business cited.

The traffic consequence is real but less apocalyptic than early headlines suggested. Pew's panel of 900 US adults found users **clicked a result in 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared, against 15% without one**. Only 1% clicked a link inside the summary ([Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)). Seer's 2026 update saw organic CTR on AI Overview queries recover from a 1.3% floor in December 2025 to 2.4% in February 2026 ([Seer Interactive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/blog/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update)). India matters here. AI Overviews launched in English and Hindi in August 2024, and AI Mode expanded to seven more Indian languages in October 2025, with India the first country outside the US to get Search Live ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/08/googles-search-live-comes-to-india-ai-mode-gets-more-languages)). No India-specific CTR study exists yet.

## Answer engines draw on two memories: what they were trained on and what they retrieve

Every engine follows the same loop. The model decides whether to search. It rewrites the prompt into one or more sub-queries, retrieves pages from an index, and cites a small subset of what it read. The engines differ mainly in which index they use and how often they search. Gemini API grounding is documented as exactly this tool call: the model "decides whether a search would help", generates its own queries, and returns citations plus the queries it ran ([Gemini API docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/google-search)). ChatGPT search uses unnamed "third-party search providers" plus partner content and its own OAI-SearchBot crawl ([OpenAI FAQ](https://help-lb.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq)). Studies disagree on how far it tracks Bing versus Google. Seer found an 87% match with Bing's top results ([parse.gl](https://www.parse.gl/blog/bing-rankings-chatgpt-visibility)); Grow & Convert found only 27% and 23% overlap with Google and Bing for the fan-out query ([Grow & Convert](https://www.growandconvert.com/ai/fan-out-query-serp-study/)). Copilot is grounded in Bing, and Bing Webmaster Tools now shows Copilot citations and the "grounding queries" behind them ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/bing-webmaster-tools-testing-new-ai-performance-report-468039)). Perplexity and Claude run their own search crawlers, PerplexityBot and Claude-SearchBot ([Perplexity](https://docs.perplexity.ai/docs/resources/perplexity-crawlers.md); [Anthropic](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8896518)).

How often an engine searches is decisive and rarely discussed. Ahrefs analysed 66.6 million free, logged-out ChatGPT responses from August–September 2026. **Only 14.4% ran a web search.** The other roughly six in seven answers came from the model's trained memory alone ([Relevant Audience summary of Ahrefs](https://www.relevantaudience.com/geo/chatgpt-query-fan-out-citations-ahrefs-study/)). Graphite saw a similar split: about 10% of logged-out ChatGPT prompts searched versus about 50% logged in ([Graphite](https://graphite.io/five-percent/demystifying-randomness-in-ai)). Visibility therefore has two layers. **Parametric presence** is being in the training data, which changes only with model releases and depends on years of accumulated web mentions. **Retrieval presence** is ranking in the right index for the engine's rewritten sub-queries, and it can move within weeks. A vendor study of 740 new pages found median time to first citation of about 4 days on Perplexity, 6 on AI Mode, 11 on ChatGPT search and 22 on Claude ([maxaeo](https://maxaeo.ai/blog/time-to-citation-ai-search/)). Treat those numbers as indicative, not benchmarks.

Crawler access is now split by purpose. That split matters because many SMB sites block the wrong bots or render content only in JavaScript. OpenAI separates OAI-SearchBot (search inclusion), GPTBot (training) and ChatGPT-User (user-triggered fetches) ([OpenAI crawlers](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)). Google-Extended controls training and grounding in Gemini apps and Vertex, **not AI Overviews**, which follow Googlebot's rules ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). Blocking training bots costs a business nothing in search. Blocking search bots, or a CDN that silently blocks them, removes it from those engines. Vercel's crawler study found that **none of the major non-Google AI crawlers execute JavaScript**. Gemini, by contrast, uses Googlebot's rendering infrastructure ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/the-rise-of-the-ai-crawler?ref=t01.li)). A client-rendered site can look fine to Google and Gemini while being an empty shell to ChatGPT, Perplexity and Claude.

### Third-party pages decide who gets recommended in "best X" answers

No vendor documents how brands are chosen for recommendation answers. Measurement tells a consistent story, though. For "best" questions, engines cite third parties, not the brand's own site. The Hoth found ChatGPT pointed to the brand's own domain in **69.6% of branded questions but only 1.3% of "best" questions** ([The Hoth](https://thehoth.com/blog/ai-cites-listicles/)). Ahrefs found recently updated "best X" listicles were the most prominent page type ChatGPT cited ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/best-lists-research/)). Across 30 million sources, Peec found Reddit, YouTube, LinkedIn, Wikipedia, Forbes, G2 and Yelp leading ([Peec AI](https://docs.peec.ai/research/most-cited-domains-in-ai-search)). The mix is volatile, however. Between March and September 2026, ChatGPT's Reddit citations fell from about one in five answers to about one in 43, while official and primary sources rose: official sources appeared in 84.4% of factual answers ([Relevant Audience / Ahrefs](https://www.relevantaudience.com/geo/chatgpt-query-fan-out-citations-ahrefs-study/)). ChatGPT's fan-out queries increasingly add "official" and "price" and use `site:` searches on brand domains. That makes a clear, crawlable services-and-pricing page a growing retrieval target, even though the recommendation itself is driven by third parties. Two further findings matter for BrandPulse's clients. LLMs disproportionately associate global brands with positive attributes ([Kamruzzaman et al., EMNLP 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13997)), which is a headwind for an independent clinic against a national chain. And no citation study yet covers Indian local queries, where Justdial (192.9 million quarterly visitors, 56.1 million listings) and IndiaMART (8.8 million supplier storefronts) are both lead channels and likely grounding sources ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/markets/stocks/story/just-dial-shares-jump-nearly-15-on-q1-fy27-earnings-details-here-542458-2026-07-13); [IndiaMART](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/INDIAMART_21072026154238_PressRelease.pdf)).

## Controlled tests deflate most GEO tactics; mentions, listings and freshness survive

The academic record is clearer than the marketing. The original GEO paper (KDD 2024) found that adding quotations, statistics and cited sources raised a source's visibility in generative answers by **up to roughly 40%**, and keyword stuffing hurt by about 9% ([Aggarwal et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3)). The gains were mostly redistribution, though: the fifth-ranked source gained up to 115% while the top-ranked source lost 30%. The test also ran without competitors applying the same methods. The NeurIPS 2025 C-SEO Bench tested the idea under competition and found most conversational-SEO rewriting methods **"largely ineffective"** and often harmful. Gains shrank as more actors adopted them, and the initial retrieval rank played "a far more dominant role" ([Puerto et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11097)). The robust lesson is that retrieval comes first and formatting second. Formatting is a congested, zero-sum edge that disappears once competitors copy it.

Schema is the best-tested popular tactic, and it failed. Ahrefs ran a difference-in-differences study of 1,885 pages that added JSON-LD against about 4,000 matched controls. Over 30-day windows it found **no detectable lift on ChatGPT (+2.2%) or AI Mode (+2.4%)** and a small, unexplained decline on AI Overviews (−4.6%). It concluded that its earlier "most cited pages have schema" correlation was confounded by overall SEO strength ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/)). The test covered only already-cited pages and a short window, so schema is not proven useless for entity clarity. It is unproven as a visibility lever. llms.txt fares worse. John Mueller said "no AI system currently uses llms.txt" and compared it to the keywords meta tag ([SERoundtable](https://seroundtable.com/google-ai-llms-txt-39607.html)), and Google's AI-features page says no AI text files are needed.

What survives has correlational, not causal, support. Across 75,000 brands, **branded web mentions correlated with AI Overview brand visibility at ρ = 0.664, against 0.218 for backlinks and 0.326 for Domain Rating**. The top quartile of brands by mentions averaged 169 AI Overview mentions, while the bottom half averaged 0–3 ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/)). Ahrefs itself calls this correlation, and big brands simply have more of everything. Freshness also holds up. Seer found about three in four pages cited by ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity had been updated within the past year, and concluded that **freshness comes from maintenance more than new publishing** ([Seer](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/study-content-recencys-impact-on-ai-visibility-in-2026)). The table below grades the tactics BrandPulse's audit and service currently touch.

| Tactic | Best evidence | Verdict for BrandPulse |
|---|---|---|
| Be retrievable: indexed, crawlable text, AI search bots and CDN allowed, server-rendered | A (C-SEO Bench); Google docs; Vercel | Core. Verify and fix. |
| Off-site mentions, third-party "best of" lists, directories | B (ρ 0.664; Hoth 1.3%) | Core service work. Causation unproven. |
| Google Business Profile completeness and reviews | Google official (local ranking; AI-features page names Business Profile) | Core for local SMBs. |
| Freshness via maintenance updates | B (Seer) | Cheap, recommend it. |
| Statistics, quotations, sources, fluent prose | A in the lab, weak under competition | Good writing hygiene. Never promise an uplift. |
| Answer-first 40–60 word blocks, FAQ hubs | D (practitioner opinion, no test found) | Reasonable tactic. Label it as such. |
| Schema (Organization/LocalBusiness) | A− null for AI citations | Keep for entity consistency. Do not score it as a visibility lever. |
| FAQ schema | Pooled in the null test; FAQ rich results restricted since 2023 | Demote to a note. |
| llms.txt | D, and Google says it is unused | Remove from the score. |
| Self-promotional "we're #1" listicles | B that they get cited; rising spam and reputational risk | Do not offer. |
| Keyword stuffing, hidden text, prompt injection, adversarial strings | A negative; explicit spam | Never. |

The honest timeline follows from the two-layer model. Listing, page and crawlability fixes can show up in retrieval-grounded answers (Gemini with grounding, AI Mode, Perplexity, ChatGPT search) within weeks. The trained-memory layer moves only with model releases. One Indian agency's benchmark of first citation wins between days 45 and 90 ([upGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/geo-aeo-pricing-benchmark-india-2026/)) is a reasonable outer bound to quote. No independently audited case study exists. The most cited one, Ramp's rise from 3.2% to 22.2% visibility after rebuilding comparison pages, is vendor-published ([The DataStory](https://thedatastory.substack.com/p/case-study-1-its-profound)).

## Reliable measurement means presence rates, repeated runs and stated error bars

The single most important measurement fact is the SparkToro and Gumshoe finding. Across 2,961 runs on ChatGPT, Claude and Google's AI features, the chance of getting **the same brand list twice was below 1 in 100**, and the same list in the same order below 1 in 1,000. The share of answers naming a given top brand, however, was fairly stable at about 55–77% ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-recommendations-change-with-nearly-every-query-sparktoro/566242/)). Rank within an answer is noise; frequency of appearance is signal. Variance persists even at temperature zero, partly because of inference infrastructure ([arXiv 2408.04667](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.04667v5)). Cited sources drift too: 40–60% of the domains cited in a month were absent the month before ([Profound](https://tryprofound.com/blog/ai-search-volatility)).

Sample-size work now gives usable rules. Graphite drew 400 responses per prompt and found that **10 runs give a visibility estimate within about 5–9 points** on average (similar for Gemini 3 Flash), and **40 runs bring 95% of prompts within 5 points**. Runs behaved like independent draws ([Graphite](https://graphite.io/five-percent/demystifying-randomness-in-ai)). Pooling across prompts works too: 50 prompts × 8 runs gives about ±5 points at the business level, and detecting a move from 20% to 30% needs about 294 answers per period ([Cloro](https://cloro.dev/blog/ai-visibility-sample-size/)). Rates should carry a **Wilson score interval**, which behaves correctly at small samples and near 0% or 100%, where the textbook interval fails ([Brown, Cai & DasGupta](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/statistical-science/volume-16/issue-2/Interval-Estimation-for-a-Binomial-Proportion/10.1214/ss/1009213286.full)). Applied to a typical BrandPulse headline, 5 of 40 answers gives a 95% range of **5.5–26.1%**, and 29 of 40 gives **57.2–83.9%**. That gap is real and survives the uncertainty. A difference of 6 versus 9 out of 40 does not. Strictly, Wilson assumes identical trials, and 40 different questions are not identical, so the interval describes "this question set". With repeats per question, a cluster bootstrap (resample questions, then runs) is more accurate and cheap to implement.

The industry has converged on four core metrics: mention rate (visibility), share of voice, average position when mentioned, and sentiment. Source metrics are reported separately, distinguishing **brand visibility** (named in the answer) from **source visibility** (the domain is cited) ([Peec AI docs](https://docs.peec.ai/metrics-overview)). Every tool's composite 0–100 score is proprietary. Ahrefs labels its own metrics "modeled" and "directional" ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/brand-radar-methodology/)), and Semrush does not publish the weighting of its score ([Semrush KB](https://semrush.com/kb/1607-ai-visibility-data)). Tracking tools run 15–350 prompts daily with repeats, priced from about $29 to $300 a month. A one-shot audit is therefore a snapshot and should say so. Tracking over time needs a frozen question set, a fixed engine and model configuration, and a multi-run baseline. It also needs weekly or fortnightly comparisons rather than daily ones, an intervention log, and **control questions** the work did not target, so that engine-wide drift is not mistaken for impact ([Graphite](https://graphite.io/five-percent/demystifying-randomness-in-ai); [Cloro](https://cloro.dev/blog/ai-visibility-sample-size/)). Business attribution is unavoidably directional. The usual signals are a GA4 custom channel built on an AI-referrer regex placed above Referral ([Terminus](https://www.terminusapp.com/blog/tracking-ai-referral-traffic/)), an AI option in "how did you hear about us" (which Ahrefs calls about as close to truth as attribution gets) ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-to-measure-ai-search-visibility/)), and branded-search lift in Search Console. Google AI Overviews and AI Mode clicks arrive as ordinary google/organic traffic and cannot be isolated.

Two Gemini-specific details affect BrandPulse's engine directly. First, the model may decide **not** to search. An ungrounded answer measures trained memory, not retrieval, so each run should log whether grounding queries fired. Second, grounding chunk URIs are `vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com` redirects, so the cited domain must come from the chunk title or a resolved redirect ([Gemini API docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/google-search); [Vertex AI docs](https://cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/grounding/grounding-with-vertex-ai-search?authuser=8)). The API answer also has no device location, personalisation or memory. It should be labelled "Gemini API with Google Search grounding", not "what customers see in the Gemini app". It is a reasonable proxy for Google's AI surfaces because it shares the search substrate, but Google has never said it equals AI Overview selection. Gemini's share of gen-AI web traffic rose from 13.6% to 25.0% in a year ([Similarweb](https://x.com/Similarweb/status/2109561054883717264)), and Jio bundles Google AI Pro free. That supports Gemini-first for India, though ChatGPT, with about 100 million weekly Indian users ([TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2026/02/15/india-has-100m-weekly-active-chatgpt-users-sam-altman-says/)), remains the larger audience.

## What BrandPulse should change: an audit that measures honestly and a service that ships proof

The KP Webbate review asked for five things. All five line up with the evidence above, and together they turn the audit from a persuasive snapshot into a measurement instrument a skeptical buyer can check. The current code shows where the gaps sit. In `src/outreach/scorecard.ts`, the overall score weights presence 40, citations 20, technical 20, content 10 and off-site 10. Inside the technical pillar, llms.txt (10 points), FAQ/HowTo schema (10), general structured data (15) and entity schema (10) make up **45 of about 135 points, roughly 6.7 points of the overall score**. In the off-site pillar, linked LinkedIn, YouTube and other social profiles make up 75 of 140 points, about 5.4 overall points. So **about 12 of 100 points ride on items the evidence rates as hype or unproven**. Meanwhile `src/promptgen.ts` instructs the local-business generator to mix in "manufacturers or suppliers", "export" markets and "OEM, private label, industrial procurement" questions. That is how a web-development agency ended up tested on fintech, manufacturing and export questions. The codebase already contains a richer path (`src/business-understanding` and `src/buyer-intent`) that extracts a business's actual services and buyers. The outreach audit should route through it.

### Positioning and pricing for India

The Indian market has two clusters. Cheap "SEO + GEO" bundles at ₹20k–₹50k a month ([TheBillionGame](https://www.thebilliongame.com/blog/aeo-pricing-india); [Adexorb](https://adexorb.com/best-geo-agency-in-india/)) sit below specialist retainers of ₹75k–₹3.5L a month with 6-month minimums. Paid one-off audits run from about ₹25k up to ₹1.5L–₹8L, and the agency-authored benchmark describes a ₹3L–₹4L three-month "minimum viable sprint" ([upGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/geo-aeo-pricing-benchmark-india-2026/)). Globally, credible agencies commit to scope and volume, not placements: "outreach volume, not guaranteed placements" ([Getspotted](https://getspotted.ai/blog/geo-agency-retainer-pricing-playbook)). None of 20 surveyed agencies states a guarantee ([Red-engage](https://red-engage.com/blog/geo-agency-pricing)). BrandPulse's free audit already undercuts a market that charges for audits. Its credibility rests on the methodology panel, so the panel is the product, not decoration.

The positioning line the evidence supports is: "We measure how often AI names you against your competitors, show you the exact answers, fix what is verifiably broken, and build the third-party presence AI draws on, then re-measure the same questions so you can see whether it moved." My inference for scoping on calls (the market data supports the bands, not these exact numbers) is a fixed-scope 6–8-week local sprint at roughly ₹30k–₹75k one-off, with optional upkeep. That sits below the specialist cluster and above repackaged SEO. For SaaS and B2B services, a 90-day sprint at roughly ₹1.5L–₹4L, then a retainer, matches upGrowth's sprint band and global entry tiers. Add the YMYL premium the market applies (+20–35%) for clinics, fintech and legal. Avoid performance-only pricing early. With answers this noisy, a 70/30 base/performance hybrid on a defined question set, as upGrowth describes, is the furthest BrandPulse should go, and only on a 6–12-month term. These prices fit the existing "no public prices, scope on the call" stance.

### Risks to design around

Google's spam policy now explicitly covers attempts to manipulate generative AI responses, and scaled content abuse is judged by intent ([Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)). BrandPulse should therefore never ship mass location pages, self-ranking "best X" listicles on client sites, hidden text or prompt-injection strings. Reported analyses show self-promotional listicles losing ground in AI Overviews after late-2025 updates ([Bushletter](https://www.bushletter.com/google-ai-overviews-cite-38-fewer-vendor-listicles-data-shows/)). Third-party listicle placements must be earned or disclosed, since paid undisclosed placement can fall under the site reputation abuse policy on the host's side. In India, the Consumer Protection Act 2019 treats a false guarantee as a misleading advertisement. The CCPA's 2022 guidelines apply to advertising agencies and carry **penalties up to ₹10 lakh, and ₹50 lakh for repeat violations** ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2022/07/07/changing-how-india-advertises-government-introduces-new-guidelines/)). "Guaranteed #1 on ChatGPT" or "recommended by AI in 30 days" in ads, proposals or the report itself is a legal liability, not just a credibility one. Fake or incentivised reviews remain actionable as an unfair trade practice, and BIS IS 19000:2022 sets a (voluntary) standard for review collection ([JSA Law](https://www.jsalaw.com/newsletters-and-updates/bis-standard-on-online-consumer-reviews/)). Health clients carry sector-specific advertising rules this research did not cover. Check them before writing clinic pages. The last risk is measurement itself. A free audit that reports single-run absences as facts is the easiest thing for a competitor or an annoyed prospect to discredit. Repeated runs and stated ranges are the cheapest insurance BrandPulse can buy.

## Conclusion

The research reframes what BrandPulse actually sells. The scarce asset in this market is not a list of GEO tactics, which controlled tests show are mostly zero-sum or null. It is **trustworthy measurement plus the unglamorous off-site work** that the strongest correlates point to. Most competitors either charge for one-shot audits or sell retainers on unverifiable "AI rankings". A free, methodologically transparent audit that a client can re-run on the same questions is therefore a sharper differentiator than any optimisation trick, and it is the same product as the paid monthly report. Building it costs mostly engineering days and roughly tripled Gemini calls, not new vendors.

Two uncertainties should shape the next quarter. First, nobody has measured citation sources for Indian local queries, or how closely Gemini API grounding tracks AI Overviews and AI Mode for them. BrandPulse's own repeated-run data across clients could become the first such dataset and a marketing asset in its own right. Second, the source mix moves by tens of points within months (ChatGPT's 2026 swing from Reddit to official sources shows how fast), so any playbook pinned to a single platform will age badly. The durable bets are being retrievable, being consistently described across the web, and being named by credible third parties. Those are also the bets that hold up if a client checks the report.
