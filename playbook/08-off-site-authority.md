# 08. Off-Site Authority: The Bigger Half of AI Visibility

On-site work makes a brand *retrievable*. Off-site work makes it *recommendable*. When an AI decides which three brands to name, it leans on what the rest of the web says: reviews, lists, forums, news, videos, directories.

**[DATA]** Ahrefs (75,000 brands): branded web mentions correlate with AI Overview visibility at 0.664; backlinks at 0.218. **[DATA]** Reddit, YouTube, Wikipedia and LinkedIn are among the most-cited domains across ChatGPT, AI Mode, Gemini, Perplexity and AI Overviews (Peec AI, 30M sources). **[DATA]** Domains with active review-platform profiles are about 3x more likely to be cited by ChatGPT (SE Ranking).

---

## 1. Start with the citation gap

BrandPulse's audit outputs the domains AI cites in the client's category. That is the off-site target list. For each domain:

| Domain type | Example | Question | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Review platform | G2, Practo, Trustpilot, Google Maps | Is the client listed? Rating? Review count vs competitors? | Claim, complete, run review engine |
| Directory | Justdial, Clutch, Capterra category page | Listed? In the right category? Complete profile? | Claim, complete, upgrade only if the paid tier is what AI cites |
| Listicle / roundup | "10 best cosmetic dentists in Delhi" on a local blog | Is the client on the list? | Outreach to the author with evidence |
| Forum / community | Reddit thread, Quora | Is the client mentioned? Positively? | Genuine participation, encourage real customers to share experiences (never fake) |
| Media | News site, trade publication | Has the client been covered? | PR with a story or data |
| Video | YouTube review or explainer | Does the client appear? | Create own videos; collaborate with creators |
| Encyclopedia | Wikipedia, Wikidata | Entry exists? Accurate? | Wikidata item; Wikipedia only if notable (independent coverage), never self-written promotional content |
| Competitor site | Competitor's own pages | Why is AI citing them? | Build the better page on our side (the "why they win" analysis) |

**Prioritize** by: how often AI cites the domain in this category x how easy it is to get on it.

## 2. The off-site playbook by channel

### 2.1 Reviews (every archetype)
- **Where:** Google Business Profile (local), plus the 1 to 3 platforms AI cites most in the category.
- **How:** ask at the moment of success (after treatment, after onboarding milestone, after delivery), with a direct link. Make it a process, not a campaign.
- **Volume target:** match or beat the median of the competitors AI names, then sustain a steady monthly flow. Recency matters.
- **Content:** ask customers to mention what they bought and why it worked ("my veneers", "the onboarding"). Never script or incentivize positive-only reviews; follow each platform's rules (incentivized reviews are banned on most).
- **Respond** to every review, especially negative ones, factually and calmly.

### 2.2 Third-party lists and roundups ("best X" articles)
The highest-leverage AI move for most businesses: AI answers to "best X" queries are often synthesized from the top few listicles.
1. Find every list AI cites for the client's top prompts (BrandPulse citation gap + manual search for "best [category] [place]").
2. Prioritize lists that rank on page 1 and are cited by multiple engines.
3. Pitch the author: a short email with why the client fits their criteria, verifiable facts (ratings, pricing, differentiators), photos/screenshots, and an offer of a demo/sample. For paid inclusion lists, decide case by case and require clear disclosure.
4. Track inclusions; recheck AI answers 2 to 6 weeks later.

### 2.3 Reddit and communities
- AI engines, especially Google AI Mode and AI Overviews, cite Reddit heavily.
- Use real, named accounts. Disclose affiliation ("I run a clinic in Saket, so take this with that bias").
- Answer questions genuinely helpfully, often without mentioning the brand. Mention it only when directly relevant.
- Monitor brand and category mentions; respond to complaints openly.
- **Never** create fake accounts, buy upvotes, or post fake reviews. It gets caught, banned and becomes a negative story AI repeats.

### 2.4 YouTube and video
- Make the video version of the top 10 buyer questions (cost, comparison, process, results).
- Titles = the question. Description = the answer + link. Chapters. Accurate transcript.
- Collaborate with creators in the category (reviews, explainers) with clear disclosure.

### 2.5 LinkedIn (B2B especially)
- Founder and team posting consistently on the category's problems, with original data and opinions.
- Company page complete and consistent with the website description.
- LinkedIn articles and posts are cited by AI for B2B queries and by Copilot in particular.

### 2.6 Digital PR and original data
- The most reliable way to earn mentions at scale: publish something worth citing. Survey, benchmark, price index, usage data, annual report.
- Pitch the headline finding to journalists and newsletters in the category with a one-page summary and charts.
- Founder commentary: respond to journalist requests (HARO-style platforms, Qwoted, Featured, local equivalents) with expert quotes.
- Local businesses: local news features, community events, awards, sponsorships.

### 2.7 Directories and citations (NAP consistency)
- List on every relevant directory with **identical** name, address, phone, website, hours, categories and description.
- India: Google Business Profile, Justdial, Sulekha, IndiaMART, Practo/Lybrate (health), Zomato/Swiggy (food), Urban Company (home services), Shiksha/Careers360 (education), Clutch/GoodFirms (agencies).
- Global: Google, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yelp, Facebook, Foursquare data partners, industry associations, chamber of commerce.
- SaaS: G2, Capterra, GetApp, TrustRadius, Product Hunt, AlternativeTo, SaaSworthy, integration marketplaces.

### 2.8 Wikipedia, Wikidata and knowledge bases
- **Wikidata:** create or complete the organization item (official website, founding date, founders, industry, HQ, identifiers, social profiles). Low barrier, used by knowledge graphs.
- **Wikipedia:** only if the business meets notability (significant independent coverage in reliable sources). Do not write your own article; conflict-of-interest editing is against Wikipedia policy and gets reverted. Earn the coverage first.
- **Crunchbase, LinkedIn, Google Knowledge Panel:** claim and keep consistent.

### 2.9 Backlinks (still matter for SEO)
Links remain a Google ranking signal and drive discovery and crawling. Earn them through data, tools, PR, partnerships, integrations, guest expertise, and local relationships. **Never buy links or use PBNs**: it is a spam policy violation and Google's link spam systems neutralize or penalize it.

## 3. The "why they win" analysis

For each competitor that AI names more than the client:
1. Which pages of theirs are cited? (BrandPulse evidence)
2. Which third-party sources mention them? Count them by type.
3. What facts does AI repeat about them? (Those are the facts their content and mentions established.)
4. What do they have that the client lacks: a pricing page, a comparison page, 5x the reviews, a Wikipedia page, a YouTube channel, presence on the top listicle?
5. Close the biggest gaps first.

---

## 4. Timeline expectations (be honest with clients)

| Lever | Typical time to show in AI answers |
|---|---|
| Fixing crawl blocks / rendering | Days to weeks (after recrawl) |
| New or rewritten pages | 2 to 8 weeks (index + retrieval), faster with IndexNow on Bing/Copilot |
| GBP improvements and reviews | 2 to 8 weeks for local answers |
| Getting onto cited listicles and directories | 2 to 6 weeks after the third-party page is updated and recrawled |
| Brand mentions influencing model "memory" | Months, tied to model retraining cycles |
| Wikipedia / major media | Months to years |

Live retrieval moves fast; training-data memory moves slowly. That is why we measure monthly across many prompts and engines, and why the first wins usually come from technical fixes, money pages and third-party listings.
